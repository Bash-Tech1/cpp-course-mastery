When you don't understand a concept:
    Explain this concept from first principles.

    Start with the problem that motivated its existence.
    Then explain how it works, why it works,
    and when I should use it.

    Give me examples and challenge my understanding
    with a few questions.


When you have a design question:
    I have the following design problem.

    Don't immediately give me the implementation.

    Help me explore different approaches.
    Explain the trade-offs, advantages, disadvantages,
    and the reasoning behind choosing one approach.

    Challenge my assumptions.


When you finish a problem:
    Review my solution as a senior C++ developer.

    Don't rewrite my code immediately.

    First evaluate:
    1. Correctness.
    2. Edge cases.
    3. Logic.
    4. Efficiency.
    5. Readability.
    6. Design quality.

    Identify the most important improvements
    and explain why they matter.

When you make a mistake:
    I made this mistake.

    Explain the underlying misconception,
    not just how to fix the code.

    Give me a small exercise to verify
    that I won't make the same mistake again.
